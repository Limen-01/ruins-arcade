# Approved v1.2 wave and machinery revision

This document supersedes conflicting gameplay requirements in v1.1 and the historical v1.0 handoff. Preserve unrelated five-stage, UI, input, art, accessibility, persistence and PWA behavior.

Stage families, in order: crossbow arrows, local lightning, beast-head lane flame, local ground fire, then mixed arrangements using those four families. There are no playable spikes, boulders or beams. Stages end only on death. Stages 1–4 show stage and cumulative scores with one-tap Next Stage. Stage 5 shows all scores, total and best total with whole-run Retry.

A wave is a finite schedule of events with one ID, one archetype and one score. Sequential waves introduce warnings successively, grouped waves announce a coherent set of simultaneous or batched events, and discrete waves contain one or simultaneous events without appended warnings. Internal events can overlap. Finish every damaging event before the next wave starts warning, then allow a perceptible recovery beat. A fully survived wave scores exactly one point after its final damage interval; death at the same simulation step wins. No partial credit. Keep simple waves at all pressure levels.

Stage effective play time advances only while the simulation is playing a real stage. It includes recovery, and excludes tutorial, pause, countdown, death and menus. It resets at every new stage. Pressure is the smooth bounded function `1 - exp(-stageTime / 30)`. Snapshot all scheduling parameters and projectile classes when a candidate wave is constructed. Warning, fire occupancy, arrow traversal, sequence spacing and recovery are independently tuned. Movement remains 0.12 seconds per released swipe, with one buffered command and no invulnerability.

Initial target parameters: first wave after 0.7 seconds; recovery `0.30 + 0.60 * (1 - pressure)` seconds; sequence spacing `0.13 + 0.17 * (1 - pressure)` seconds. Warning leads are independently configured per family and remain above the validator's reaction allowance. Arrow flights are slow 0.80, medium 0.52 and fast 0.34 seconds; late play raises fast frequency but retains slow arrows. Complexity weights vary continuously from 75/20/5 to 10/35/55 percent for simple/moderate/advanced. Exact measured behavior and rejection rates belong in `PROGRESS.md`.

Crossbow direction markers stay outside the playable grid and no full-lane ground warning is shown. Slow, medium and fast arrows have distinct charge animation cadence and source details. Lightning builds localized charge then strikes down, with a short damaging impact matching the visible bolt. Ground fire has local ignition warning, sustained upward flame, and a clear extinguishing phase. Lane flame remains sourced from the beast-head at the arena edge. Local cues mark targets without revealing safe answers. All visuals remain readable with sound muted and reduced motion enabled.

Deaths are non-gory. Crossbow retains arrow carry/knock-away. Lightning chars the hero, forms a small ash pile and drops a stylized head with one bounce. Both fire families char the hero and emit one distinct black smoke puff above the head. Freeze scoring and gameplay on death; present results after about 1.15 seconds. Resolve simultaneous causes in a deterministic event order and play the sequence once.

Before announcement, validate the complete candidate wave against the current motion and buffered input using time-aware continuous collision, reaction allowance and swipe cadence. Replay every accepted witness through `Simulation`. Announced geometry, speed and timing are immutable. Bound generation and validate every fallback. Rejection must preserve the previous state and retry without concealing long idle gaps. No inter-wave overlap is introduced for pace.

Verification covers family order, archetypes, finite timing, wave scoring, smooth time pressure and reset, speed variants, lightning/fire intervals, fairness and replay, death animation timing and cause, stage reset, settings, pause, reduced motion and production offline reload. Capture phone-size timed frames for all new effects, wave beats and death presentations. Physical-device feel and human enjoyment remain pending until tested.
